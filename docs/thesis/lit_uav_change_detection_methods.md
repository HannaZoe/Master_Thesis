# Literature review: UAV/LiDAR DEM differencing methodology for detecting small-scale (doline-scale) geomorphic change

Compiled 2026-09-28. Scope: methodology for multi-temporal UAV-SfM/LiDAR DEM differencing applied to small (dm–m scale) geomorphic features, precedent studies on karst/doline UAV surveys, and known pitfalls of short-interval, small-magnitude change detection. Prepared to support the Zugspitzplatt collapse-doline thesis (4 epochs: Oct 2024, Jun 2025, Aug 2025, Aug 2026; native ~5 cm resolution).

---

## 1. Core DEM-differencing uncertainty methodology

### 1.1 Wheaton, J.M., Brasington, J., Darby, S.E., & Sear, D.A. (2010). "Accounting for uncertainty in DEMs from repeat topographic surveys: improved sediment budgets." *Earth Surface Processes and Landforms*, 35(2), 136–156. DOI: 10.1002/esp.1886.

**Summary:** The foundational paper for spatially variable DEM-of-Difference (DoD) uncertainty. Introduces two methods to move beyond a single global "minimum level of detection" (LoD) threshold: (1) a fuzzy inference system (FIS) that estimates spatially variable elevation uncertainty per-DEM based on factors such as point density, slope, and survey method quality, producing a per-cell uncertainty surface rather than one blanket ± value; (2) a spatial-coherence filter that adjusts uncertainty estimates based on whether erosion/deposition patches are spatially coherent (real geomorphic signal tends to cluster; noise tends to be spatially random). Change is then thresholded at a user-chosen confidence interval (commonly 95%, i.e. propagated 2σ combined uncertainty of the two epochs: LoD = sqrt(σ1² + σ2²) × t-value). Underpins the widely used GCD (Geomorphic Change Detection) software (Utah State University / Queen Mary University of London, https://gcd.riverscapes.net).

**Relevance/actionable guidance:** This is the baseline method the thesis should cite and likely apply (or explicitly justify deviating from) for any raster-based DoD approach. Key actionable points: (a) do not use a single flat LoD for the whole 5 cm DEM — build a spatially variable uncertainty surface if possible (point density, local slope/roughness, and registration precision all vary across the Zugspitzplatt plateau, and steep doline walls will have systematically higher uncertainty than the flat plateau surface); (b) always threshold DoD at an explicit, stated confidence level rather than reporting raw differences; (c) 95% CI thresholding can suppress real but small change — the paper itself flags that legacy, spatially uniform error models routinely over- or under-state actual uncertainty, which is exactly the risk at doline scale.

### 1.2 James, M.R., Robson, S., & Smith, M.W. (2017). "3-D uncertainty-based topographic change detection with structure-from-motion photogrammetry: precision maps for ground control and directly georeferenced surveys." *Earth Surface Processes and Landforms*, 42(12), 1769–1788. DOI: 10.1002/esp.4125.

**Summary:** Extends spatially variable uncertainty from raster DoD into full 3-D point-cloud space for SfM-photogrammetry-derived surveys specifically (as opposed to Wheaton's TLS/raster-oriented framing). Produces 3-D "precision maps" describing photogrammetric and georeferencing uncertainty across the surveyed volume (this uncertainty is highly non-uniform depending on camera network geometry, GCP distribution, and viewing angles — not just terrain roughness), then feeds these into an adapted M3C2 (Multiscale Model to Model Cloud Comparison) point-cloud comparison for confidence-bounded 3-D change detection. Note: I could not retrieve the full text (Wiley paywall, ResearchGate blocked WebFetch, and the two open-access PDF mirrors — UCL Discovery and White Rose eprints — returned as unparseable binary/FlateDecode streams rather than extractable text), so the specific cm-level LoD numbers and worked examples in the paper are not confirmed here; only the abstract-level method description above is verified. The paper is directly retrievable as open-access PDF at https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/112711/1/James%20et%20al%202017%20DEM.pdf or https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10060986/ for manual reading.

**Relevance/actionable guidance:** Because this project is SfM-photogrammetry-style UAV survey data (not TLS), this paper's framing — that uncertainty is a function of camera/GCP geometry, not just point density/roughness — is more directly applicable than Wheaton's TLS-oriented FIS. Actionable: the thesis should build (or at least discuss) a 3-D precision map reflecting where GCPs are dense/sparse across the Zugspitzplatt survey area, since precision will not be uniform, and doline interiors (fewer usable tie points, steep walls, possible shadow) are likely to be among the worst-constrained parts of the model — precisely where the change signal of interest lives.

### 1.3 Lague, D., Brodu, N., & Leroux, J. (2013). "Accurate 3D comparison of complex topography with terrestrial laser scanner: Application to the Rangitikei canyon (N-Z)." *ISPRS Journal of Photogrammetry and Remote Sensing*, 82, 10–26.

**Summary:** Original M3C2 algorithm paper. M3C2 compares two point clouds directly (not gridded rasters) along the local surface normal at each "core point," using a cylindrical neighborhood; local surface roughness (quality of a planar fit within that cylinder) is used to estimate noise, and this is combined with registration error to produce a spatially variable, statistically defensible level of detection at each point, without interpolation to a raster.

**Relevance/actionable guidance:** M3C2 (and its successor M3C2-EP, which explicitly propagates point-level registration/error-propagation uncertainty — Winiwarter et al., *ISPRS J. Photogramm. Remote Sens.*, 2021, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2021.06.011) is the current best-practice alternative to raster DoD, especially for steep or vertical surfaces such as doline sidewalls where raster-based DoD (which assumes single-valued z per x,y cell) breaks down. Given that dolines have overhangs/steep walls at their scale, a point-cloud-based M3C2 approach (implementable in CloudCompare, free, or the py4dgeo Python library) is likely preferable to pure raster DoD for at least the doline interior/rim zones, even if raster DoD is used for the broader plateau change map.

### 1.4 Li, P., Li, D., Hu, J., Fassnacht, F.E., Latifi, H., Yao, W., Gao, J., Chan, F.K.S., Dang, T., & Tang, F. (2024). "Improving the application of UAV-LiDAR for erosion monitoring through accounting for uncertainty in DEM of difference." *Catena*, 234, 107534. DOI: 10.1016/j.catena.2023.107534.

**Summary:** Develops a fuzzy-inference-system DoD-uncertainty model driven by local point density and surface roughness/topography, validated against independent TLS-derived erosion/deposition volumes. The uncertainty model explained >96% of raw DoD uncertainty variance. Notably, even after this refinement, the method "did not well capture subtle erosion" — i.e., the smallest-magnitude real changes remained the hardest to separate from noise, and the model showed a systematic tendency to underestimate volumetric change magnitude at the fine end.

**Relevance/actionable guidance:** Directly cautionary: even a well-validated, purpose-built uncertainty model built specifically to detect erosion still struggled with subtle/small-magnitude change. For the thesis, this is evidence that the smallest real doline-scale changes (a few cm of subsidence, small ravel/collapse at doline rims) are exactly the class of signal most likely to be swamped by residual DoD uncertainty, and volumetric change estimates should be expected to be conservative (biased toward underestimating small real changes) rather than over-detecting.

## 2. Precedent: repeat/multi-temporal UAV or LiDAR applied to karst dolines/sinkholes

No study was found that performs true multi-epoch, differencing-based change detection on natural collapse dolines at the Zugspitzplatt's scale and resolution. All karst-specific UAV/LiDAR precedents found are single-epoch morphometric mapping studies, not change-detection studies. This is itself an important finding (see synthesis).

### 2.1 Kim, J., & Hong, I. (2025). "Analysis of Doline Microtopography in Karst Mountainous Terrain Using UAV LiDAR: A Case Study of 'Gulneomjae' in Mungyeong City, South Korea." *Sensors*, 25(14), 4350. DOI: 10.3390/s25144350. Open access, PMC12297937.

**Summary:** DJI Matrice 300 RTK + Zenmuse L2 LiDAR, Penta Return mode, 150 m AGL, ~98 pts/m² point density, final DEM at 0.25 m resolution (coarser than this thesis's planned ~5 cm). RTK-GNSS gave ~0.006 m horizontal and ~0.008 m vertical positional accuracy for the survey itself, but the authors state final effective precision for morphometric measurements is limited by the 0.25 m DEM grid (±0.25 m linear, ±1–2 m² areal). Seven dolines characterized morphometrically (area, perimeter, axes, elevation profiles). The only "temporal" comparison was against a 2013 1:5000-scale topographic map (not a repeat UAV survey); two previously mapped dolines were not re-identified, ambiguously attributed to either real infill/cultivation or to detection-method artifacts from the old low-resolution map — the authors could not distinguish real change from mapping-method artifact even at decadal, whole-doline-disappearance scale.

**Relevance/actionable guidance:** Closest topical precedent (UAV LiDAR + individual doline microtopography), but resolution (25 cm) is 5x coarser than this project's target and it performs no rigorous multi-temporal differencing — it is a mapping paper, not a change-detection paper. Its own inability to confidently attribute a lost doline to real change vs. artifact, even between very different vintage/resolution datasets 11 years apart, is a useful cautionary data point for the thesis: distinguishing artifact from real change is already hard at whole-feature-disappearance scale, let alone at cm-scale subsidence within a single doline.

### 2.2 Kim, J., & Hong, I. (2024). "Evaluation of the Usability of UAV LiDAR for Analysis of Karst (Doline) Terrain Morphology." *Sensors*, 24(21), 7062. DOI: 10.3390/s24217062. Open access, PMC11548729.

**Summary:** Same authors/region (Danyang karst, South Korea), same UAV LiDAR system, ~197 pts/m², 0.25 m DEM. Compares UAV LiDAR DEMs, UAV photogrammetry DSMs, and 1:5000 topographic maps for doline detection across four study areas. LiDAR detected 13 dolines vs. 21 for photogrammetry (higher false-positive rate under vegetation) vs. 0 for the topographic map. Single-epoch only; no change detection performed.

**Relevance/actionable guidance:** Reinforces that LiDAR outperforms pure photogrammetry for doline detection specifically because of vegetation/canopy penetration — relevant if any part of the Zugspitzplatt dolines have vegetation or snow-patch cover complicating bare-earth extraction in some epochs (e.g., late snowmelt differences between the Jun 2025 and Aug 2025/2026 epochs could introduce a systematic, season-dependent bare-earth extraction difference that is not "real" doline change).

### 2.3 Ferreira, C.F., Hussain, Y., & Uagoda, R. (2023). "UAV-based doline mapping in Brazilian karst: A cave heritage protection reconnaissance." *Open Geosciences* (De Gruyter), DOI: 10.1515/geo-2022-0535.

**Summary:** UAV-based doline mapping at fine spatial resolution over a ~49 km² area (Goiás/Bahia, Brazil) to support cave-heritage and groundwater vulnerability assessment. Single-epoch mapping/classification study, not change detection; full text was not directly retrievable (405 error on direct fetch), so this entry is based on abstract-level search snippets only, not a full read.

**Relevance/actionable guidance:** Confirms UAV-based doline mapping is an active, recognized approach internationally, but again offers no repeat-survey/change-detection precedent. Flagged as unread in full — worth the student re-checking directly if citing beyond this summary.

### Note on absence of alpine/karst-specific repeat-survey precedent
No paper was found that repeats UAV/LiDAR surveys over the *same* individual dolines/sinkholes across multiple epochs to quantify dm-scale surface change through DoD/M3C2 (the closest analogues are (a) sinkhole *collapse event* monitoring — see below — which tracks a single, large, unambiguous new feature over days–months, a very different, much-higher-signal regime than this thesis's dm-scale change on *pre-existing stable* dolines over 1–2 year gaps; and (b) generic small-scale geomorphic/erosion change-detection methodology from non-karst settings, §1 and §3). This is a genuine literature gap the thesis should explicitly name — it strengthens the thesis's novelty claim but also means there is no directly transferable "this precision worked for dolines" benchmark; the student must adapt thresholds from fluvial/hillslope/erosion-monitoring literature and, ideally, empirically derive project-specific LoD from stable-ground control areas on the Zugspitzplatt itself.

Cover-collapse sinkhole monitoring precedent (different regime, useful as contrast): repeat UAV/TLS surveys of an actively forming cover-collapse sinkhole in Pasco County, Florida, tracked collapse area growing from 1,395 m² (day 4) to 1,626 m² (day 32) using M3C2 point-cloud comparison (source: USF Sinkhole Conference 2020 proceedings, found via search only, not fetched in full — cite with caution). This is a fast, large-magnitude, unambiguous-signal collapse event, not comparable in precision requirements to this thesis's slow, dm-scale, stable-doline monitoring.

## 3. Pitfalls: apparent change as processing/registration artifact, especially at short (1–2 yr) baselines and small scales

### 3.1 de Haas, T., Nijland, W., McArdell, B.W., & Kalthof, M.W.M.L. (2021). "Case report: Optimization of topographic change detection with UAV structure-from-motion photogrammetry through survey co-alignment." *Frontiers in Remote Sensing*, 2, 626810. DOI: 10.3389/frsen.2021.626810. Open access.

**Summary — the single most directly actionable methodological paper found.** Nine repeat UAV-SfM surveys (DJI Mavic 2 Pro, 100 m AGL, 2.5 cm GSD — close to this thesis's ~5 cm) of the Illgraben debris-flow torrent, Swiss Alps. Compares three processing strategies: (1) "classical" — each epoch processed independently with its own GCPs; (2) co-alignment without GCPs — all epochs bundle-adjusted together; (3) co-alignment with GCPs. Under classical independent processing, some epochs showed vertical offsets up to 0.97 m (vs. a "typical" classical offset of 0.15 m) relative to stable terrain — i.e., pure processing-induced apparent elevation change of up to ~1 m in places with **zero real ground change**, large enough to completely swamp any dm-scale doline signal. Co-alignment (bundle-adjusting all epochs' imagery together rather than independently) reduced relative vertical error by a factor of 3–4 and horizontal error by a factor of ~2; mean vertical offset dropped from 0.25 m (classical) to 0.06 m (co-alignment+GCPs), horizontal from 0.17 m to 0.09 m. The authors explicitly frame this as a warning: naive independent per-epoch processing is a major, under-recognized source of spurious "change" in multi-temporal UAV-SfM change-detection studies, and recommend co-alignment as standard practice, notable also because it is available in standard SfM software (Agisoft Metashape) with no special tools required.

**Relevance/actionable guidance — high priority for the thesis methods chapter:**
- This is a load-bearing citation for the methods section. The thesis MUST NOT process the four epochs (Oct 2024, Jun 2025, Aug 2025, Aug 2026) as four independent SfM projects and then difference the resulting DEMs — that is exactly the "classical" workflow this paper shows can introduce apparent offsets an order of magnitude larger than a doline-scale signal.
- Recommended approach: co-align/bundle-adjust across epochs (or at minimum use a fixed, unchanging network of GCPs/stable-terrain tie points common to all four surveys, and verify residuals on stable, non-doline ground between every epoch pair before trusting any DoD in the dolines themselves).
- Concrete validation step the thesis should adopt: report the DoD/M3C2 result on deliberately stable, flat, non-karst reference areas outside the dolines for every epoch pair, exactly as this paper's "48 stable terrain features" validation did — any nonzero "change" there is the empirical noise floor and should set the LoD, not a generic literature value.
- The fact that this paper is about a very different setting (debris-flow torrent) but with a very comparable GSD (2.5 cm vs ~5 cm here) and short/irregular epoch spacing makes its quantitative offset numbers a realistic worst-case-if-done-naively benchmark for this project.

### 3.2 Groos, A.R., Aeschbacher, R., Fischer, M., Kohler, N., Mayer, C., & Senn-Rist, A. (2022). "Accuracy of UAV Photogrammetry in Glacial and Periglacial Alpine Terrain: A Comparison With Airborne and Terrestrial Datasets." *Frontiers in Remote Sensing*, 3, 871994. DOI: 10.3389/frsen.2022.871994. Open access.

**Summary:** Assesses UAV-SfM accuracy across four high-alpine sites, directly relevant environmental analogue to the Zugspitzplatt (glacial/periglacial, high alpine, seasonal snow). RMSE ranged from 15 cm (best case: small 0.2 km² area, dense GCPs at 40/km²) to 135 cm (worst case: large 2.5 km² area, sparse GCPs at 10/km²). Key accuracy-degrading factors identified: (a) low-contrast/texture-poor snow surfaces impair SfM feature matching (bare rock/scree with sun-exposure or surface roughening retains matchable texture, clean fresh snow often does not); (b) insufficient image overlap (<70%, vs. recommended 70–80%) causes model distortion; (c) sparse/uneven GCP distribution causes systematic positioning errors, worse in rugged terrain; (d) on steep slopes, small horizontal positioning errors of only a few decimeters translate into up to ~1 m of vertical error — a horizontal-to-vertical error amplification effect on steep terrain. Authors explicitly recommend either full raw-imagery reprocessing together or rigorous co-registration on stable tie points to avoid compounding errors between epochs.

**Relevance/actionable guidance:** Directly transferable environmental context. Two specific risks for this project: (1) the Jun 2025 epoch may have partial snow cover on/around the Zugspitzplatt (a high-elevation karst plateau with typically late snowmelt) while Oct 2024, Aug 2025, and Aug 2026 likely do not — this creates a real risk of a systematic, snow-driven bare-earth/surface discrepancy between epochs that is not real doline morphological change, and the thesis should explicitly document snow cover conditions at each survey date and consider excluding or flagging any epoch pairs with incomplete snow-free coverage inside dolines; (2) doline walls are inherently steep, and per this paper's finding, that is exactly where horizontal registration error gets amplified into large vertical error — meaning doline sidewalls, not doline floors, may be the noisiest part of the whole DoD, requiring either exclusion from quantitative floor-lowering estimates or separate (higher) uncertainty bounds.

### 3.3 General short-baseline change-detection risk (secondary/synthesized sources, lower confidence — found via search snippets, not independently full-text verified; use with care and re-verify before citing in the thesis)

Multiple secondary search results (not independently fetched in full) converge on the same generic point: for 1–3 year intervals specifically, "changes are often subtle... easily masked by sensor noise, atmospheric differences, or illumination changes," and comparing independently-processed multi-temporal UAV products risks small geometric mismatches, radiometric/shadow differences, and local artifacts being misread as terrain change — reinforcing (not independently corroborating beyond) the de Haas et al. and Groos et al. findings above. Treat this paragraph as directionally consistent with, but not independent additional evidence beyond, §3.1–3.2.

## Ranked synthesis and recommended precision/uncertainty budget for this project

**Ranked by direct actionability for the thesis methods chapter:**

1. **de Haas et al. (2021)** — most important single paper. Forces a specific methodological decision (co-align across all 4 epochs; do not process independently) with quantified stakes (up to ~1 m spurious offset if done wrong, comparable GSD to this project).
2. **Groos et al. (2022)** — most important for environmental realism. High-alpine RMSE realistically ranges 15 cm (best case, dense GCPs, small area) to well over a meter (sparse GCPs, large area); steep-slope error amplification and snow-texture issues are directly applicable to Zugspitzplatt doline sidewalls and the June epoch's likely partial snow cover.
3. **Wheaton et al. (2010)** and **James, Robson & Smith (2017)** — the methodological backbone for how to compute and propagate uncertainty (spatially variable LoD, 3-D precision maps, confidence-thresholded DoD) rather than using one flat number; James et al. specifically because this is SfM, not TLS, data.
4. **Lague, Brodu & Leroux (2013), M3C2** — recommended computation method, especially for doline sidewalls/rims where raster DoD's single-valued-z assumption breaks down.
5. **Li et al. (2024)** — cautionary evidence that even sophisticated, validated uncertainty models still underdetect the smallest/subtlest real changes — sets expectations low for what magnitude of doline change will be confidently detectable.
6. **Kim & Hong (2025, 2024)** and Ferreira et al. (2023) — establish that doline-specific UAV/LiDAR mapping is a recognized, precedented approach, but none perform genuine repeat-survey change detection; this is a real literature gap the thesis fills, not a technique the thesis can simply copy.

**What precision/uncertainty this project should expect to plan for:**

- Do not assume the native ~5 cm data resolution translates into ~5 cm (or better) change-detection precision. Realistic literature-grounded vertical uncertainty for UAV-SfM DEMs in high-alpine, partly steep, partly textureless terrain, even with good GCP practice, is more plausibly in the **10–30+ cm range** (Groos et al.'s best case was 15 cm RMSE only with dense GCP coverage on a small, favorable site), and could be worse on doline sidewalls specifically due to slope-driven error amplification.
- Processing-induced (not ground-truth) apparent change between independently processed epochs can plausibly reach **tens of cm to ~1 m** if epochs are not co-aligned/bundle-adjusted together (de Haas et al.) — this is a methodological choice fully within the thesis's control and should be treated as a mandatory safeguard, not an optional refinement.
- Given this, the project's practically detectable change signal (post-thresholding at a defensible confidence level, e.g. 95% CI per Wheaton et al.) for doline floors is likely on the order of **several cm to ~10–20 cm** at best under a rigorous co-aligned workflow with dense, stable, well-distributed GCPs verified on non-doline stable ground — and larger, effectively uncertain, on doline sidewalls/steep rims.
- This means the smallest end of the target feature range the thesis states (~30 cm features, decimeter changes) sits close to or only modestly above the realistic noise floor, especially over the shorter (~2-month, Jun–Aug 2025) and even the ~1-year epoch gaps. The thesis should budget for (a) an empirically-derived, per-epoch-pair LoD computed from the actual survey data's own stable-ground residuals (not assumed from resolution alone), (b) co-alignment/joint bundle adjustment across all four epochs as standard workflow, (c) separate/wider uncertainty treatment for steep doline walls vs. floors, (d) explicit documentation and, where feasible, correction for snow-cover differences between epochs (especially the Jun 2025 survey), and (e) a stated expectation that some real but very small (few-cm) doline changes may be statistically indistinguishable from noise and should be reported as such rather than as false positives.
